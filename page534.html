#8035. Trump Won’t Betray Britain Over the Falklands

📅 21 Oct 2026 | Category: other | Source

In his second term, Donald Trump has aggressively pursued an America First foreign policy. And rightly so. The U.S. president represents the American people, and every policy he advances should be in his nation’s interest. But backing British sovereignty over the Falkland Islands fits firmly within this vision, and his administration should stand shoulder to shoulder with the UK on an issue of huge importance to the British people.

Last month, in response to questions from British media, President Trump declared that the U.S. would review its long-standing policy of neutrality on the Falklands issue. This was immediately seized upon by Argentina’s president Javier Milei, a close ally of Trump, who announced tougher sanctions against foreign companies with plans to drill for oil around the Falkland Islands, as well as a renewed push to end British sovereignty.

The U.S. president has also seemed to equivocate over the question of whether the U.S. would come to Britain’s aid in the event of a future war between London and Buenos Aires. Trump made it clear he was hugely disappointed in the refusal of the British Government to back U.S. military action against Iran.

>>> Trump Will Never Forgive Burnham if the Chagos Deal Is Resurrected

Having worked closely with the U.S. administration in opposition to Labour’s disastrous Chagos surrender deal, I have witnessed first-hand the White House’s immense frustration with Britain’s socialist government, which has frequently been at odds with Trump’s team, from the Iran war and Israel to net zero, mass migration and freedom of speech. The Trump administration has been particularly unhappy over Labour dragging its feet on a firm commitment to swiftly increase defence spending in line with Nato commitments made at the Hague summit last year.

This is the tensest relationship that I have seen between London and Washington in nearly a quarter of a century in the D.C. policy world. The reality is that Labour simply isn’t trusted, and the renewed push on Chagos by No 10 under Andy Burnham on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly further underscored this. The British Government has been acting as its own worst enemy.

But I do not believe the Trump presidency will ultimately shift the U.S. position on the Falklands, despite all the stupid actions of the Labour Government. Here’s why.

The Anglo-American special relationship still matters inside the Washington Beltway, in both the executive branch and on Capitol Hill. The King’s visit to the U.S. in April was a stunning success, with even “No Kings” Democrat Members of Congress giving King Charles a series of standing ovations when he spoke to a joint session of Congress.

President Trump’s extremely warm reception for the King at the White House and his speech welcoming the King at the arrival ceremony, which I attended, were a glowing tribute to the enduring strength of our two great nations. The bottom line is that President Trump loves Britain, the British people and his British heritage through his Scottish mother. And like Trump, nearly 60 million Americans have British ancestry according to the 2020 census, including much of America’s political and economic leadership.

>>> How the U.S.-Europe Rift Got Balkanized

Throw into the mix the massive levels of trade and investment between the two countries, the vital strategic importance of the U.S./UK base at Diego Garcia, the key role played by RAF Fairford in Gloucestershire (despite the decision to temporarily remove U.S. stealth strategic bombers in the wake of an Iranian terror plot), and the extremely close intelligence ties between the CIA and MI6, and the special relationship remains very real.

Not a single American official I have spoken with in the last few months has told me they support a change in U.S. policy over the Falklands. They understand the huge importance this issue has to the British people, and all of them speak of their admiration for Margaret Thatcher who ordered the liberation of the islands from Argentina following the brutal invasion by its military junta in 1982.

U.S. officials also acknowledge that the Argentine position over the Falklands was viewed sympathetically by the Obama administration when Hillary Clinton was Secretary of State and far-Left Cristina Kirchner was Argentina’s president. Republicans are reluctant to take a stance that Democrats have traditionally favored in Washington, and which would be championed by the American Left.

There remains immense goodwill in the United States towards Great Britain and the British people, and very little desire to throw America’s closest ally under the bus over the Falklands. It is firmly in the U.S. national interest to defend the vital principles of sovereignty and self-determination the American people cherish.

#4574. EA Is Closing Down Legendary Studio BioWare Amid More Job Cuts

📅 21 Oct 2026 | Category: other | Source

Electronic Arts plans to eliminate roughly 400 positions in an upcoming round of job cuts, per a new report from Bloomberg, and will also be closing the doors on its long-time role-playing game maker BioWare.

Per Bloomberg's Jason Schreier, these cuts represent about three percent of EA's total staff, and roughly 50 at BioWare. A letter sent to staff today from EA's COO David Tinson and Chief Studios Officer Cam Weber said that they've taken a "hard look at our business" and will cut projects, including the next Mass Effect.

"As we focus on these larger opportunities, we've had a small team at BioWare working on early-development for Mass Effect," the letter reads. "We've made the decision not to move forward with this development, which means we’re closing the studio and our Edmonton workspace. BioWare is a legendary studio that helped define the role-playing genre, and we do not take this decision lightly."

2020 trailer screenshot for the now-canceled 5th Mass Effect

Sports, shooters (like Apex Legends), and Star Wars (including the next Jedi title) will continue apace at Electronic Arts. The publisher also has a new Iron Man game in the works, and plans are in the works for more Battlefield, as well as a new collaboration between Respawn and DICE. The staff letter also suggests a planned Sims game will be put on ice in favor of another venture, and that while Skate development will continue, EA will downsize the team working on it.

EA was fully acquired earlier this year by a number of investor groups, including Silver Lake, Affinity Partners, and the Public Investment Fund of Saudi Arabia, taking the publicly traded company private. In its announcement at the time, EA chairman and CEO Andrew Wilson said, "We're entering this next chapter from a position of strength with partners who share our vision and ambition. Together, we'll invest boldly, accelerate innovation, and build the next generation of games and experiences for the hundreds of millions of players and fans who inspire us every day."

A statement from BioWare posted to social media shared the closure as well, highlighting the list of games it has brought to life: Baldur's Gate, Neverwinter Nights, Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic, Dragon Age, Mass Effect, and Anthem. The team's focus now, per the statement, is on supporting one another through this transition.

"What generations of BioWare built together is something to celebrate and remember," the statement reads. "These games became so much more because you played them, debated your choices, fell in love with the characters, and made these worlds your own. From all of us at BioWare, thank you for being the most meaningful part of our story."

 

BioWare has been known throughout its history for exactly that: an undoubtedly legendary run of role-playing games where choice, characters, and moments matter. From the early era of D&D-driven adventures to leading the pack in console RPG development with KOTOR, Mass Effect, and Dragon Age, few Western RPG studios boast the legacy BioWare had.

Still, BioWare often sailed on troubled waters. The underwhelming releases of Mass Effect: Andromeda and Anthem tarnished the studio's pristine image in the eyes of some fans. Dragon Age: The Veilguard, the studio's most recent—and final—game was generally better received by critics, but spent a decade in development turmoil. Internal strife over the making of these massive RPGs was the subject of frequent reporting. The term "BioWare magic," referring to how the studio's games would finally come together in the twilight hours of development, became a sticking point as the years went on.

There is no denying, though, how BioWare has played an active hand in molding the role-playing genre over the years. Even a recitation of past works doesn't feel accurate to the impact; the influence of Mass Effect and Dragon Age alone is something we still see ripples of today. Even as other studios have taken up the torch and put their own spin on companion-driven, choices-matter stories, there is still an innate sense of "BioWare" that's hard to remove from those interactions and moments.

Layoffs have become distressingly commonplace, as cuts to legendary teams are announced at a never-ending pace. But to break news writing decorum, I had always hoped, throughout years of working in this industry, that I wouldn't ever have to eulogize a studio like BioWare. A place that shaped my own dive into video games, and I'm sure did the same for many, many others. A roof under which developers worked long hours to craft stories we'd carry with us for years. I'm thankful for that, and hope those affected find somewhere new that's rewarding of their talents.

#7935. Burkina Faso Restores Death Penalty

📅 21 Oct 2026 | Category: other | Source
Click to expand Image Burkina Faso soldiers patrol aboard a pickup truck on the road from Dori to the Goudebo refugee camp, on February 3, 2020. © 2020 OLYMPIA DE MAISMONT/AFP via Getty Images

Burkina Faso’s unelected parliament has unanimously adopted a new penal code that restores the death penalty for high treason, terrorism, acts of espionage, parricide and importation of expired or prohibited products. The country had abolished the death penalty for ordinary crimes in 2018 while its last known judicial execution took place in 1988.

Reinstating capital punishment sends a chilling message to critics of the military junta and risks becoming another tool to suppress dissent. Since the September 2022 military coup, Burkina Faso’s junta has steadily dismantled civic and political rights. Authorities have banned media outlets, suspended unions and civil society organizations, abolished multiparty politics, and subjected critics to arbitrary detention, enforced disappearances, unlawful conscription, and torture.

Justice Minister Edasso Rodrigue Bayala defended the decision to reinstate the death penalty as a necessary measure to combat terrorism. He said that armed groups exploited the previous law’s leniency by promising new recruits they would face no more than life imprisonment if convicted of terrorist acts.

Human Rights Watch opposes all capital punishment because of its inherent cruelty. The unfairness of death sentences in Burkina Faso would be exacerbated by the country’s ill-equipped justice system. All terrorism-related cases are handled by the Specialized Judicial Unit Against Terrorism-Related Crimes, established in 2017. Human Rights Watch documented in April the special court’s slow investigations and serious procedural failures that undermine defendants’ fair trial rights.

A former terrorism court judge once explained to me that judges sometimes kept suspects in detention despite insufficient evidence, fearing the military might summarily execute them if released. He described the dilemma as “better to violate the right to liberty in order to preserve the right to life.” With the death penalty’s return, even that grim calculation may no longer hold.

Reversing course on the death penalty runs counter to a broader international movement toward abolition. The United Nations General Assembly and the African Commission on Human and Peoples’ Rights have urged states to establish moratoriums on executions, narrow the scope of capital punishment, and progressively reduce the number of offenses punishable by death with a view to its eventual abolition. 

Restoring capital punishment will not resolve Burkina Faso’s security crisis. In a system already plagued by serious due process failures, the death penalty merely heightens the risk of irreversible injustice.
