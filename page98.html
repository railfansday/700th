#31202. Entrepreneurship might be the key to a longer life

📅 26 Oct 2026 | Category: other | Source

If you thought entrepreneurship was too stressful a path to venture down, you might want to reassess. A new study found that there may be a surprising benefit to starting a business: longevity. 

The study, titled “Do Entrepreneurs Live Longer?,” comes from researchers at the School of Business at Stevens Institute of Technology in New Jersey and was published in the Journal of Business on October 22. For the research, associate professor Cristiano Bellavitis and his collaborators examined 20 years of data from 27,501 Americans 50 and older.

Interestingly, it found that those who work for themselves may live longer than those who spend the bulk of their lives working for others: Entrepreneurs had a 10% lower risk of premature death. That was true even after researchers accounted for other factors that might tip the scale in either direction, such as age, income and wealth, and level of education.

The research offers a different perspective on work that can feel all-encompassing or lead to longer hours, which entrepreneurship is often associated with. While previous studies have found that long hours can lead to an uptick in death by heart-related illness, such as stroke, other considerations apparently come into play, such as the sense of freedom that can (sometimes) flow from working for yourself. 

Bellavitis said it’s a vital component that showed up in the study. “Most people think of entrepreneurship as extremely stressful, and it certainly can be. But entrepreneurship also gives people a high degree of autonomy and the opportunity to pursue something they genuinely love or care about,” he stated in a press release. “Our findings suggest those benefits may have lasting effects.”

Entrepreneurs are known to experience burnout in high numbers. However, the study revealed there was one major consideration that made the difference: The greatest advantage happened when that autonomy was the most present. “What seems to matter is not entrepreneurship alone, but the circumstances under which people enter it,” Bellavitis says. 

For example, when entrepreneurs launched businesses out of desire rather than necessity, longevity showed up. That means that, no matter what your profession—and whether you’re working for yourself or someone else—choice seems to matter deeply.

Longevity might be the biggest payout, but it wasn’t the only one the study spotted. Entrepreneurs reported lower levels of both depression and loneliness. While those are two mental health factors that can contribute to better health, they can also contribute to a greater sense of happiness. 

Therefore, entrepreneurship—at least when pursued out of passion and purpose rather than need—might not just be the key to a long life, but also a happy one.

📅 26 Oct 2026 | Category: other | Source

The big picture: Brazil denied Trump a close ally at the helm of Latin America's largest economy, snapping a streak of seven straight conservative wins in the region. In Europe, Serbia and Hungary toppled leaders with close Trump ties who had dominated their countries for years.

  • Another massively pivotal election looms Tuesday, when Trump ally, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, will face a battle for political survival.
  • On the eve of the election, Netanyahu touted Trump's endorsement and his "friendship" with the American president. But as the global right has made gains, opposition forces proved this weekend that they still have fuel.

Driving the news: In Brazil, leftist President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva staved off a challenge from Flávio Bolsonaro, the son of former President Jair Bolsonaro.

  • The younger Bolsonaro, widely seen as the heir to his father's populist movement, refused to concede and called for the results to be annulled, according to translations.
  • The elder Bolsonaro is serving a 27-year sentence after being convicted of plotting a coup to overturn his 2022 loss. Trump has called the prosecution a "WITCH HUNT."
  • The White House did not immediately respond to Axios' request for comment on the race.

Between the lines: Brazil's election emerged as a test of Trump's political influence in Latin America, where right-wing and Trump-backed candidates have celebrated an uninterrupted series of wins.

  • Those victories include Trump-endorsed Abelardo de la Espriella in Colombia, Nasry "Tito" Asfura in Honduras and José Antonio Kast in Chile.
  • Trump did not formally endorse Flávio Bolsonaro, but he met with him earlier this year and imposed significant tariffs on Brazil as he protested the charges against Bolsonaro's father. Lula is a vocal Trump critic.
  • In March, Flávio Bolsonaro told an audience at the Conservative Political Action Conference that America faced a "fork in the road" in the election: "Either you have the most powerful ally in the hemisphere or an antagonist."

Zoom out: Meanwhile, across the Atlantic, a student-led opposition movement in Serbia toppled the right-wing party of long-serving strongman Aleksandar Vučić, who was seeking to become prime minister.

  • Unlike the Bolsonaros, Vučić conceded defeat Sunday.
  • The Balkan leader had courted Trump's inner circle, including Donald Trump Jr., and he described the president as a "kindred spirit."
  • It reflects the shifting tides that also allowed next-door Hungary to oust Trump-allied Viktor Orbán, once one of the Western world's most entrenched populist leaders, in April after 16 years in power.

Yes, but: Europe's political upheaval is cutting both ways: While the populist right has fallen in Serbia and Hungary, it's gaining ground in two of the continent's largest economies.

  • In France, far-right presidential front-runner Marine Le Pen launched her 2027 campaign this weekend, seizing on nationwide unrest to pitch her tough-on-crime, anti-migrant agenda.
  • In Germany, the far-right Alternative for Germany won a record 44% in a state election last month and continues to lead in national polls.

What we're watching: Trump's historic move of endorsing Netanyahu in Tuesday's Israeli elections will once again test his influence abroad.

  • Tuesday's election in Israel is set to be tight for Netanyahu, who has sought to elevate Trump's endorsement as he jockeys with former Israel Defense Forces Chief of Staff Gadi Eisenkot.
  • The 77-year-old Netanyahu has been prime minister for some 19 years in nonconsecutive terms. Analysts see the election as a referendum on the security failure of Oct. 7, 2023, that killed around 1,200 people.
  • Whoever governs Israel could shape Trump's next moves in the Middle East — including whether to escalate the war in Iran.

📅 26 Oct 2026 | Category: other | Source

President Trump will campaign with three football stars in Texas on Tuesday, leaning into America's favorite sport as Republicans scramble to turn out voters in the final week before the midterms, Axios has learned.

Why it matters: Trump is trying to mobilize the lower-propensity voters who helped return him to the White House in 2024 — but who may be less inclined to vote when he isn't on the ballot.

Driving the news: Former college football stars Johnny Manziel and Colt McCoy, along with two-time Super Bowl champion Danny Amendola, will join Trump for campaign appearances in Lubbock and Corpus Christi.

  • The appearances bring some of Texas' biggest football names onto the campaign trail in a state where the sport commands an enormous following.
  • Amendola starred at Texas Tech in Lubbock, Manziel won the Heisman Trophy at Texas A&M, and McCoy was a standout quarterback at the University of Texas.

The big picture: Trump has made football a recurring feature of his campaign stops in recent weeks.

  • He attended the Texas-Tennessee game in September and planned to attend the Georgia-Alabama showdown earlier this month before canceling because of a hurricane.
  • Former NFL coach Jon Gruden joined Trump at an Ohio rally this month, while former Georgia and NFL star Todd Gurley accompanied the president on a stop in Georgia.
  • Vice President Vance, a lifelong Cincinnati Bengals fan, visited the team's practice facility last week while campaigning in Ohio.
  • Republicans are also pouring millions into ads during NFL and college football games.

What they're saying: "Trump has many bases. And football is one of them, college and pros," said an adviser. "The response is good. And the president loves the atmosphere. So why not?"

Between the lines: Football offers Trump a way to reach younger men who helped power his 2024 victory — a constituency Republicans are struggling to energize in the midterms.

By the numbers: Polls suggest Republicans face a challenge holding onto Trump's gains among young men.

  • Trump won men under 30 by 14 points in 2024, according to a Tufts University analysis. But a Harvard Youth Poll released last week found Democrats leading Republicans by 26 points among young men who say they will definitely vote in November.
