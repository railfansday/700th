#4371. Xbox Wanted A Gargantuan Paycheck To Partner With Netflix On TV And Movies

📅 23 Oct 2026 | Category: other | Source

Video game adaptations are all the rage right now, with movie and TV projects in various stages of development, and Netflix is looking to strike while the proverbial IP iron is hot. The streaming giant already has a deal in place with Microsoft to develop a Gears of War movie, and it's looking to grab more Xbox IP to build new franchises. The only problem? Microsoft's price tag was reportedly quite a bit more than Netflix was willing to pay.

That's according to entertainment journalist Matthew Belloni, who wrote in a Puck News report that Netflix had offered Microsoft $500 million for seven years to exclusively license Xbox titles for film and TV. Xbox CEO Asha Sharma and the brand's business affairs team countered by demanding $800 million, prompting Netflix to rescind the offer.

While Netflix has declined to comment on the report, Xbox told Belloni it had received "record-breaking interest" in its IP and still values Netflix as a partner on upcoming projects.

Belloni added that Xbox is now "pursuing smaller deals with more partners," like the Diablo animated series under development at Netflix, another season of Fallout at Prime Video, the Call of Duty movie at Paramount, and a sequel to A Minecraft Movie at Warner Bros. Pictures. Xbox is also expanding its multimedia ambitions; earlier this month, it announced Xbox XP. This new arm of the company, devoted to movies, TV, merchandise, theme parks, and live events, will be headed by Lucasfilm veteran Kayleen Walters.

Xbox Pictures is one of its subdivisions, and it'll develop film and television projects, as well as other forms of "linear storytelling" using Xbox IP.

#4372. Cancelling Mass Effect And Closing BioWare Was An Obvious Thing For EA To Do, Experts Say

📅 23 Oct 2026 | Category: other | Source

Electronic Arts just confirmed a major shakeup that included the closure of BioWare and cancellation of its upcoming Mass Effect game. Closing the studio and cancelling the project was a horrible thing for the people who worked there and fans who enjoy BioWare games and were looking forward to the next Mass Effect. But taking that action was no surprise whatsoever from a business sense.

Video game analyst Joost Van Dreunen told IGN that EA needed to make drastic cuts to service the $18 billion in debt that it has taken on due to the leveraged structure of its sale to Saudi Arabia's PIF, Silver Lake, and Affinity. Interest payments alone on that debt are about $1.8 billion annually, which exceeds how much money EA makes in annual EBITDA ($1.5 billion).

Van Dreunen said BioWare getting shut down was "entirely predictable and precisely what a leveraged buyout looks like once the deal closes."

"In that context, a 50-person team working on a game that is years from launch is the easiest line to cut, regardless of its legacy,” van Dreunen said.

Piers Harding-Rolls of Ampere Analysis, meanwhile, said the closure of BioWare is "emotive" for people who loved BioWare and its 30-year legacy of making popular and well-liked RPGs, but the math just didn't math.

Dragon Age: The Veilguard failed to meet sales expectations, and Mass Effect: Andromeda and Anthem were commercial disappointments, too. This contributed to an environment where EA management would look to kill the studio outright. He said EA was likely already considering big changes for BioWare prior to the acquisition even closing.

“Unfortunately, the studio’s recent big-budget releases have not performed well enough commercially," he said. "The changing nature of high-end gaming and the need to cut costs means that it is likely that BioWare’s future was under discussion before EA was acquired by the PIF and its partners.”

Avi Lewis, a Canadian politician, has called on the government to help save BioWare by providing "federal support" to the workers to buy it back.

"This is private equity, this is what it does," he said. "Buy up businesses, run them into the ground, and rip them apart for short-term profit. The developers at BioWare know how to run the studio. They know how to make the games we all love. They should be given the first opportunity--and the federal support--to buy it and run it themselves."

The new Mass Effect game was revealed in 2020. Since then, the game remained in "early development" until its cancellation this week. BioWare released two games after the new Mass Effect was announced, including Mass Effect Legendary Edition in 2021 and Dragon Age: The Veilguard in 2024.

A trailer for the next Mass Effect game leaked after BioWare's closure was announced. While that game is not going ahead, the Mass Effect TV show for Prime Video remains in development.
