#29791. Lula beats Bolsonaro to win Brazil presidential election

📅 26 Oct 2026 | Category: other | Source

Brazil President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva was elected to a fourth, nonconsecutive term in the nation's presidential election, according to early results released Sunday evening.

Why it matters: Lula's victory deals a setback to Brazil's right-wing populist movement and preserves a major counterweight to President Trump's influence in Latin America, with tensions over trade and tariffs threatening to persist.

  • Lula, the incumbent candidate, was facing Flávio Bolsonaro, the son of former Brazilian President Jair Bolsonaro. Flávio was widely seen as the heir to the nation's right-wing populist movement.

The latest: Lula thanked the Brazilian people after his election win, but Bolsonaro told reporters he would not accept the results and said they should be annulled, according to translations.

The big picture: The election has been shaped by the impact of U.S. tariffs, climate change and the Amazon rainforest, 60% of which is in Brazil.

  • Bolsonaro campaigned on public security and tackling corruption, presenting himself as a continuation of his father's political legacy, while Lula emphasized defending Brazilian sovereignty against U.S. pressure.
  • Yet substantive policy debate was limited, with both candidates focusing instead on corruption allegations levied against the opposing camp, wrote Matthew Taylor, a Brazil politics expert at American University.

Zoom in: After the U.S. imposed additional 25% tariffs on some Brazilian goods this summer, Lula accused Bolsonaro of being "Trump's candidate" and the U.S. president of wanting to "colonize" Brazil.

  • Lula has enacted climate policies aimed at reversing environmental deregulation under the previous administration, while Bolsonaro denied climate change and pledged to continue his father's political legacy.
  • Amazon deforestation has fallen 71% since 2022, the year before Lula returned to office, reaching its lowest annual level on record in 2026. Yet Lula's environmental record is complicated by his support for expanded oil development.
  • Lula defeated Jair Bolsonaro in the previous election. The elder Bolsonaro is now serving a 27-year prison sentence for plotting a coup to overturn the 2022 election, though he remains under house arrest due to health problems.

#35724. James Packer pays $1.8m to law firm also defending Netanyahu’s corruption charges

📅 26 Oct 2026 | Category: technology | Source

Updated October 26, 2026 — 3:49pm,first published 12:24pm

Billionaire James Packer has paid at least $US1.25 million ($1.78 million) to the Tel Aviv law firm also defending Benjamin Netanyahu against allegations the Israeli prime minister corruptly received benefits from the Australian businessman and other wealthy supporters a decade ago.

An investigation by this masthead on the eve of seismic Israeli elections has uncovered a money trail involving monthly $US50,000 wire transfers over the past two years from a bank account controlled by Packer’s company to the account of Netanyahu’s legal defence firm.

The first monthly “retainer” payment from the Citibank account of Packer’s firm Consolidated Press International was made on September 23, 2024, to the account of law firm Hadad Roth Shenhar at Israel’s Mizrahi Tefahot bank.

The monthly payments, which Packer has not denied, have continued for two years. A source aware of the payments, but not authorised to speak publicly, said they should be urgently investigated to determine whether Netanyahu was receiving an undisclosed benefit from Packer as the law firm defends the Israeli prime minister over allegations he unlawfully accepted expensive gifts from Packer between 2014 and 2016.

Israeli authorities have repeatedly blocked Netanyahu from receiving funds from wealthy backers to pay for his defence in his long-running corruption case. This masthead has not confirmed that Netanyahu benefited from the payments.

If it was found Packer’s payments to the law firm directly or indirectly benefited Netanyahu, the source alleged the payments could involve fresh breaches of Israeli laws governing how politicians can accept support, and might also fall under US laws that govern financial transactions or possible benefits foreign politicians receive from businesses.

Asked about the allegations on Monday morning, a spokesperson for Netanyahu said: “I couldn’t give a monkey’s about your questions.

“We are very busy. We have an election on right now.”

When contacted by this masthead on Friday night, Packer did not deny the rolling $US50,000 payments but declined to comment.

A source familiar with the 59-year-old former media mogul’s financial dealings later insisted the wire transfers to Netanyahu’s lawyers were for work done for Packer – including “deal finding” in Israel where Packer has business interests – and unrelated to the prime minister’s court case.

This masthead subsequently requested evidence of any deals in Israel or work undertaken by the law firm, but neither Packer nor his company have yet responded.

In addition to running Netanyahu’s defence against claims the Israeli leader broke his country’s law by accepting luxury goods from Packer and wealthy film producer Arnon Milchan, law firm Hadad Roth Shenhar is helping Netanyahu contest allegations that he improperly traded favours with an Israeli newspaper publisher and telecommunications company.

Netanyahu’s primary defence attorney is Hadad Roth Shenhar’s most senior lawyer, Amit Hadad. Hadad has gained prominence by appearing for several Israeli government ministers and business figures, but Netanyahu is his most notable client.

Hadad did not respond to questions from this masthead.

In addition to the ongoing corruption trial, Netanyahu has controversially, and unsuccessfully, sought legal permission allowing his wealthy overseas friends to fund the law firm Hadad Roth Shenhar.

Israeli authorities have ruled Netanyahu cannot rely on the rich backers because this would amount to a breach of trust and conflict of interest.

Transparency International said Packer’s decision to pay $US1.25 million to the law firm defending Netanyahu demanded urgent and independent scrutiny.

“As there are already significant corruption charges against Mr Netanyahu relating to past gifts from Mr Packer, and other business associates, these new allegations must be thoroughly investigated by authorities,” said Transparency International’s Australian director, Clancy Moore. Moore also cautioned that evidence might yet show the payments were unrelated to Netanyahu.

Moore stressed scrutiny was also required in light of the previous concerns about who could pay for Netanyahu’s legal fees.

“Significantly, Israeli authorities have repeatedly ruled that Mr Netanyahu can’t use funds from foreign benefactors to fund his legal defence against corruption charges due to conflict of interests and his failure to disclose his wealth,” Moore said.

Packer found himself entangled in Netanyahu’s corruption case after gifting the prime minister champagne and cigars more than a decade ago, but has not been accused of any wrongdoing and has not been called as a witness to the prime minister’s corruption trial.

Packer and Netanyahu at the time lived in neighbouring villas in the exclusive township of Caesarea and were regular guests at each other’s homes. Netanyahu even had a key to Packer’s luxury property.

Packer once called the Israeli prime minister “probably the most impressive man I’ve ever met”.

Australian Federal Police officers interviewed Packer on behalf of their Israeli counterparts in late 2017 but stressed he was a witness in the case – not a suspect – and that he co-operated fully.

This masthead submitted detailed questions to Packer’s privately owned Consolidated Press Holdings (CPH). The company did not reply.

The questions included whether it was appropriate for Packer to make payments to Netanyahu’s law firm given it was defending him in a case which involved the Australian businessman, and why Hadad Roth Shenhar was selected for investment advice.

Consolidated Press International (CPI) is one of several private investment arms operated by Packer. At the time that payments to Netanyahu’s law firm began, CPI had more than $600 million in investments in technology companies including Taiwan semiconductor Nvidia and Facebook owner Meta.

Packer later pivoted to gambling stocks through large stakes in Las Vegas-based poker machine manufacturer Light and Wonder, and Sportsbet’s parent company Flutter. He has also invested heavily in AI.

Tuesday’s election in Israel represents a crucial juncture for the Middle East, with Netanyahu’s right-wing government facing potential defeat. His removal from office would trigger a reset of the conflicts in Iran and Gaza and redraw the relationship between Jerusalem and US President Donald Trump.

Netanyahu – Israel’s longest-serving prime minister and the country’s first sitting leader to be charged with a crime – has repeatedly invoked the security situation in the Middle East to argue for delays in giving evidence at his corruption trial.

Known as Case 1000, Netanyahu was in 2019 indicted on fraud and breach of trust charges for accepting luxury gifts from Packer and film producer Arnon Milchan worth about $295,000. Netanyahu is also accused of intervening on behalf of Milchan for various business matters, including a US visa renewal.

The 77-year-old prime minister completed his evidence on these charges and other unrelated allegations in July. The court is yet to deliver a verdict.

Milchan gave evidence via video link in 2023 and told the heavily fortified courtroom in Tel Aviv: “I know that Packer really wanted to be connected and endear himself to Netanyahu.”

The Israeli leader and Packer became estranged after the corruption allegations first emerged in 2016 and did not talk for up to eight years. However, the pair reconnected around the time Netanyahu addressed the US Congress in July 2024, when Packer sat in the viewing gallery as a special guest. They later shared a Shabbat dinner.

The first payment by Packer to the PM’s law firm was made eight weeks after the two rekindled their friendship.

The prime minister earlier this year asked Israeli President Isaac Herzog to be pardoned to end the long-running legal saga, claiming the trial was “undermining national unity”, and that a pardon would help Israel “tap new opportunities with the US”.

Trump endorsed the request during a speech to the Israeli parliament in October last year. “This has been one of the great wartime presidents,” Trump said. “Cigars and champagne, who the hell cares about that?”

The US president subsequently wrote to Herzog, restating the case for Netanyahu’s pardon, and labelled the corruption case a “political, unjustified prosecution”.

London: Thomas Markle’s ex-wife was the author of a letter to the UK’s Metropolitan Police that accused him of being a paedophile.

The Duchess of Sussex’s father was accused of child sex offences in a letter sent to the force in 2019, which it admitted it did not act on.

The letter, a copy of which has been seen by the London Telegraph, was sent by Roslyn Loveless, who was married to Markle from 1964 to 1975 and is the mother of Samantha and Thomas, Meghan’s half-siblings.

Scotland Yard said it had been sent a letter in 2019 “containing a number of claims about alleged offending” outside its British jurisdiction, but it had not shared the letter with any overseas authorities.

The force is considering whether to report itself to the regulator for its handling of the letter, which was shared with Prince Harry and Meghan, the Duke and Duchess of Sussex at the time.

As first reported by the New York Post, Loveless, who died in 2024, wrote the letter in 2019.

In the letter, she wrote that Markle had allegedly told her he went to the Philippines and Thailand “because 12-year-olds are legal there”. Markle has denied all the allegations against him, and said that he has never had sex with anyone under the age of 18.

Just after Harry and Meghan’s son Prince Archie was born, Loveless gave a rare interview in which she said that Markle was an “awful father” and should not be allowed to see his grandson.

The couple divorced in 1975, four years before Markle married Doria Ragland, Meghan’s mother.

It emerged last week that police in the Philippines were investigating Markle, 82, for allegedly possessing “hundreds, if not thousands” of child sexual abuse images found in recent months.

His son, Thomas Markle Jr, is said to have handed a dossier of evidence to the Philippine authorities.

On Sunday, the Philippines’ interior secretary, Jonvic Remulla, said it appeared Markle was a “sex offender of the grossest kind”, although further forensic analysis was needed to be definitive.

He confirmed Philippine police were investigating a laptop found alongside images of child abuse at Markle’s former home.

Remulla said the country planned to take “appropriate legal actions” against Markle if sufficient evidence was found.

Speaking to the UK Guardian, he said: “In these cases where family disputes are mixed up, we have to consider all the angles. Right now all signs point to a possible sex offender of the grossest kind.

“We want to be sure before we release the findings. The forensic data will be complete in a week.”

Since news of the claims against Markle broke, Meghan has confirmed that she had been aware of separate allegations since January 2019, when the Met passed her a letter containing “troubling and disturbing” information about her father.

The Met said the letter contained “a number of claims about alleged offending outside the United Kingdom”, which was sent to the royal protection team “to raise concerns in relation to the security of the Duchess of Sussex”.

They decided to share it with Harry and Meghan, but “details of the allegations in the letter were not shared with other law enforcement agencies”.

It is understood that Meghan was told not to discuss the letter and had believed that it was being investigated.

Thomas Markle snr has strenuously denied all the allegations against him, telling the Mail On Sunday: “I have never had sex with anyone under the age of 18.”

It has emerged that when Meghan wrote to her father while he was in hospital last year, she accused him of being unsafe around children, saying he would never be permitted to meet her own and urging him to seek professional help.

In an interview this week, Markle, who has had a leg amputated and has dementia, said: “I am not well enough to go to the Philippines but I will speak to anyone who wants to talk to me.

“I have nothing to hide. I am an innocent man and she gave me no chance to defend myself. I have been asking to see my grandchildren before I die.

“If she saw this awful letter ... why did she not contact me or send someone to speak to me?

“She has the power to send the police or FBI. Why wait eight years? Why cut me off for a letter which contains lies and not let me defend myself?”

The FBI said it was “aware of the allegations and will provide support, as appropriate, to the Philippine authorities”.

“We will also investigate any related allegations of federal violations of US law,” a spokesman added.

Samantha Markle, 61, came out in defence of her father on Sunday, telling The Daily Mail that if her mother sent the letter to the police it was out of “hatred and bitterness”.

She described her as a chronic alcoholic who was violent and abusive, adding: “If my mother wrote that letter then she should not be considered credible.

“She was bitter that Dad left her and had a successful career in Hollywood. There was a lot of hatred and bitterness there,” she said.

“She never ever once mentioned to me that she thought Dad was a paedophile.”

Thomas Markle snr said that he did not believe his ex-wife had written the letter, describing it as a “set-up”.
