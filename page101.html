#31204. Scientists say something in the air could be quietly damaging your brain

📅 26 Oct 2026 | Category: other | Source

In the 1980s, an egg sizzling in a frying pan warned us what our brains looked like on drugs. Today, scientists are just starting to uncover what our brains look like on plastics and pollution. Spoiler: It’s also not great.

Recent research reveals that our brains are becoming increasingly littered with plastic particles. In fact, The Washington Post reported last week that several new studies show a similarly alarming impact from polluted air.

One study, published in April in The Journal of Nutrition, Health, and Aging, identified evidence that pollutants are linked to increased dementia risk and reduced brain volume. The researchers further identified a connection between air pollution and accelerated biological aging, which is linked to a higher dementia risk. Another study found that inflammation may enhance dementia risk in the presence of elevated exposure to a particular type of pollutant.

Combined, the studies could provide further clues about the association between exposure and dementia, as The Washington Post reported. The additional research could help to solve some of the mysteries outlined last year by a team of researchers from Rush Alzheimer’s Disease Center and other institutions. In a study published in Frontiers in Toxicology, the researchers sought to better understand how these particles had penetrated deep into the brain tissues of 11 adults who died between the ages of 80 and 91.

But pollution and inhalation likely aren’t the first point of entry for these plastics. In recent years, scientists have discovered that microplastics are present in human placentas and that babies are born with these particles in their organs, meaning that exposure is all but inevitable. As we grow, these particles add up through daily ingestion from our food, water, and plastic packaging.

Consequently, there may be a cumulative effect across a lifetime of exposure to pollutants, as Caleb Finch, a professor emeritus at the University of Southern California’s Leonard Davis School of Gerontology, told The Washington Post. “There’s a lot we don’t understand yet, but we do know that the risk of dementia increases in proportion to the amount of air pollution.” 

DEMENTIA, POLLUTION RISKS

The link between pollutants and dementia risks adds an interesting wrinkle to other recent research, particularly as the number of people with dementia is expected to more than double by 2050. A study published in JAMA Neurology found that women and people who live in low- and middle-income countries already have higher rates of dementia, though researchers couldn’t pinpoint an exact reason why.

The authors of that study didn’t specify the countries with higher rates of dementia. The five countries deemed the most polluted, according to a ranking by IQAir, are Pakistan, Bangladesh, Tajikistan, Chad, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

While the U.S. is comparatively clean by global standards, it does have a high incidence of dementia—roughly 1 in 9 people over the age of 65 are living with the condition, according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. 

As more research unveils clues about the association between dementia and pollution, that connection may add another layer to existing public health concerns—particularly given the Trump administration’s practice of granting exemptions to the Clean Air Act. During his second term alone, The New York Times reported on Sunday that President Trump has exempted 202 facilities in 39 states and Puerto Rico from clean-air regulations that were set to take effect, including some of the country’s worst industrial polluters.

#31205. NBA partners with Kalshi and Polymarket as official prediction markets

📅 26 Oct 2026 | Category: other | Source

The NBA has struck deals that will make Kalshi and Polymarket the league’s preferred prediction markets, along with an agreement with a federal agency tasked with regulating those exchanges.

Those agreements, announced Monday, are intended to provide “significant integrity protections for NBA-related markets,” including limitations on what can be offered on those exchanges, transparency when it comes to suspicious patterns by site users, and banning league and team personnel from making trades related to league events.

The prediction markets will have “information-sharing obligations relating to investigations and suspicious trading,” the NBA said. The NBA has had similar guardrails in place with its sportsbook partners for some time and has cited instances where that system has flagged irregularities in betting patterns.

“If you’re going to be betting on our games or trading on our games or playing fantasy around our games, it’s better for it to be regulated than unregulated,” said Scott Kaufman-Ross, the NBA’s head of media distribution and partnerships. “And clearly, our fans have interest in this activity. So, we’re in a better position to protect the integrity of our games in a regulated market where there’s transparency, where things can be monitored and we have insight into what’s happening.”

The NBA is also expanding its agreements with current official sports betting partners, such as DraftKings, to include their prediction-market offerings. Not all states offer legalized sports betting but predictions markets—at least on some level—are essentially available in all 50 states.

More than a dozen states are involved in litigation over prediction markets, and earlier this month the NFL asked the Supreme Court to rule on the regulation of such markets. The U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission contends that it has exclusive jurisdiction on oversight, though some states believe it should fall under their purview.

Earlier this month, the NBA told sportsbooks they will not be permitted to offer certain types of wagering this season, including but not limited to which players record the first stat of a game, the outcome of the opening tip, and taking the “under” involving players not expected to play more than a few minutes. The deals announced on Monday are expected to have similar restraints.

“A big part of this is a limitation on the types of markets that can be offered,” Kaufman-Ross said. “We will have the right to approve the types of markets that are available on our games, which is a very important component for us. And we’ve had very constructive dialogues with the companies as part of that.”

The NBA’s memorandum of understanding with the CFTC “reflects a shared commitment to ensuring that NBA-related prediction markets are subject to appropriate regulatory oversight and integrity protections,” the league said.

Kalshi and Polymarket, as part of their partnerships with the NBA, will now be able to describe themselves as the “Preferred Prediction Market Exchange of the NBA,” plus have access to league intellectual property such as logos, be able to get NBA statistical data from league partner Sportradar “to operate and settle authorized NBA-related markets,” and other marketing activations.

The terms of the NBA’s agreements with Kalshi and Polymarket were not disclosed, other than the league confirming that they are for multiple years. There are NBA stars with business relationships with those markets; Miami’s Giannis Antetokounmpo has promoted Kalshi and Philadelphia’s LeBron James recently signed a deal with Polymarket. At least one other NBA star, San Antonio’s Victor Wembanyama, recently said he will never endorse such sites.

—By Tim Reynolds, AP basketball writer
