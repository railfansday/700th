#35773. Blue Cheese Dressing

📅 21 Oct 2026 | Category: technology | Source

This is my classic Blue Cheese Dressing, a great all-rounder that I use for “everything”. It’s a dip, it’s a dressing, it’s a sauce! It’s loose enough to toss through your favourite salads, yet thick enough to cling to anything you dip into it. I can’t wait to show you how I make a big steak salad with this!

I know blue cheese is a little divisive but I’m firmly in the love-it camp! Especially in dressing / dip form. Not because the bold blue cheese flavour is mellowed, but because it becomes endlessly useable, beyond just cutting off huge hunks of cheese to balance on bread.

In this dressing form, it is thick enough so it clings to anything you dip in it (veggie sticks, buffalo wings, wedges), creamy enough for tossing through salads, and saucy enough for spooning over steak and drizzle over pizza.

Just give me an excuse to use it and I will!

Here’s what you need to make this. If you’re a seasoned blue cheese eater, I recommend roquefort for its bold, beautiful blue cheese flavour. If you’re a newbie feeling a bit nervous, start with Blue Castello. Blue cheese training wheels!

Blue cheese – Any creamy blue cheese will work, just don’t use the hard crumbly sort as they won’t melt into the dressing. As for strength of blue cheese flavour, here’s a list of common ones from the strongest to mildest:

Roquefort – Sharp, tangy, salty, creamy and complex. My favourite!

Stilton – strong, rich and savoury, but rounder and less sharp than Roquefort.

Danish Blue / Danablu – classic blue-cheese flavour. This is a good value easy to find choice.

Gorgonzola Piccante – fairly strong and spicy, though creamier and less salty.

Bleu d’Auvergne – medium-strong, creamy and earthy.

Gorgonzola Dolce – mild, creamy, sweet and buttery with gentle blue flavour.

Blue Castello – most mild, good one for beginners! This is also easy to find, sold at grocery stores an suburban corner stores here in Australia. We love a Blue Castello!!

Buttermilk – This is the main liquid in the dressing that thins it into a dressing consistency. Thicker than milk, thinner than sour cream, with a lovely tangy flavour. 🙂 If you’d rather not get a whole carton just for this recipe, you can use a 50/50 mix of milk and yogurt instead.

Mayonnaise and sour cream – These add richness and keeps the dressing a nice thick, creamy consistency. Whole-egg mayonnaise is best, the flavour is smoother and not as sweet or tangy as regular mayonnaise can be. Kewpie is also great here!

Garlic powder – This is the only flavouring I use in blue cheese dressing, I personally feel that anything more is unnecessary as the blue cheese already adds so much flavour. I prefer using powder to fresh as it’s more earthy and suits the blue cheese flavour better. If you substitute with fresh, leave the dressing for a few hours to let the flavour mellow.

Apple cider vinegar – Just a touch of acidity. We don’t need too much as the buttermilk already provides some. You can substitute with lemon juice.

Worcestershire sauce (OPTIONAL) – Just a small 1/2 teaspoon (do not want to taste it), I personally feel it’s not needed if using a blue cheese with strong flavour but I’d add it if using a mild blue cheese (like blue castello).

Blue cheese dressing is supposed to have some little lumps of blue cheese in it. But if you prefer to make it completely smooth, you can use a stick blender instead.

Mash the blue cheese with a fork until it sort of becomes crumbly. Best to use a fork rather than fingers as it’s creamy (unless you’re just looking for an excuse to suck your fingers clean of blue cheese!).

Whisk with the remaining ingredients until it becomes a thick creamy dressing. Whisk vigorously for 10 to 15 seconds until most of the blue cheese blends into the dressing. As mentioned above, it won’t be completely smooth, there will be some little lumps of blue cheese which is normal – everybody loves the little pops of blue cheese!

And that’s it. Your blue cheese dressing is ready to use!

Its uses extend far beyond the basic role as a salad dressing! Here are just a few ideas:

Salad dressing – Toss it through salads or drizzle over iceberg lettuce wedges, like pictured above!

Dipping sauce – Buffalo wings, veggie sticks, crackers, potato chips (plain), potato wedges, chicken fingers, Fish Fingers, Popcorn chicken, bread.

Drizzle – on pizza (especially Buffalo chicken pizza!), fries, baked potatoes, roasted potatoes, smashed potatoes, roasted vegetables, a big show-off Tomahawk Steak (or a regular one)

Fellow blue cheese lovers, tell me – what are some other ways to use blue cheese dressing?? I wanna know! – Nagi x
