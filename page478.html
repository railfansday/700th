#8032. Families and Students Need Guidance on AI

📅 22 Oct 2026 | Category: other | Source

Delaware families and policymakers are wrestling with the same issue troubling Americans nationwide: how to handle artificial intelligence.

In 2024, lawmakers in the Blue Hen State created a committee to study AI. Good thing, because parents and teachers badly need guidance on this issue.

Yet AI technology has advanced rapidly over the past two years, so the committee should prioritize drafting recommendations for K-12 schools.

To date, state officials have set only “voluntary” guardrails around AI use in elementary and secondary schools, according to the Delaware-based Caesar Rodney Institute. In May, legislators considered a proposal to create a pilot program that would allow 12 schools to use AI technology.

>>> What’s Really Behind the Call to Regulate AI

Still, the final report on the activities would not be due until 2029, and this year’s freshman class will be seniors by then. These students will be gone before lawmakers act on the report’s findings. Instead, lawmakers should focus on helping students in school today.

Besides, given the pace of change in AI technology, this proposal is too little, too late.

Fortunately, legislative models already exist that lawmakers can apply to allow the use of AI in schools, protect student data and prohibit nonacademic AI activities in classrooms.

Heritage Foundation research explains that lawmakers need not explicitly ban or allow the use of AI in schools. For school officials who allow AI in classrooms, however, educators should adopt training programs for teachers and students. These programs should help teachers and students draft prompts and questions for generative AI bots; create media using AI, such as audio, video and pictures; edit student writing; detect biases in AI results; evaluate different AI bots to decide which are the most effective; and protect student privacy.

Local school boards should adopt policies stating that students may use AI for schoolwork only when specifically allowed by teachers.

The U.S. Department of Education is urging state and local authorities to develop solutions. “Research and experience have demonstrated that technology is a valuable instructional tool when it supports strong pedagogy and effective teaching practices,” writes Kirsten Baesler, assistant secretary for elementary and secondary education. “The U.S. Department of Education believes these decisions [about education technology] are best made by states and local communities.”

Policymakers should move quickly. Even if self-reported student usage numbers are slightly inflated, the share of students using AI remains remarkable.

Education officials in large school systems such as New York City and Los Angeles are attempting to prohibit the use of AI for this school year. With so many students already using bots, however, a ban on AI will be difficult to enforce.

>>> Meta Abandons Its AI-Generating Tool, but Public-Safety Guardrails Are Needed

Instead of prohibiting AI, policymakers should draw on existing research to select appropriate educational applications of the technology. For example, lawmakers should block the use of companion bots that simulate personal interactions and develop relationships with users.

Instead, educators can give students access to generative bots that use data to summarize complex information. Teachers can benefit from AI programs that help organize lesson plans. When teachers assign students to use AI, students should report how they used the technology.

Parents should have the choice to opt out of these assignments and have students complete the work using other methods.

State policymakers must focus on boosting student outcomes as technology evolves. Federal officials agree. Ms. Baesler writes: “The question should not be whether teachers and students use technology. The question should be whether the technology we ask teachers and students to use improves learning and students’ academic outcomes.”

As Delaware’s AI committee drafts recommendations, officials should focus on student success. Those on both sides of the political aisle—and on either side of AI debates—can agree on this.

Then, parents and teachers can share best practices and offer solutions to help students across all 50 states.

#36133. From Forest to Mill: Sharing Knowledge Across the Wood Supply Chain

📅 22 Oct 2026 | Category: technology | Source

National Forest Products Week | October 18–24, 2026

The forest products industry is built on connections. From the forests where timber is grown and harvested to the trucks transporting raw materials and the mills manufacturing everyday products, each part of the wood supply chain plays an essential role. Connecting the people, businesses, and expertise across this supply chain is central to FRA’s mission.

During National Forest Products Week, FRA celebrates everyone who makes this industry possible. It is also an opportunity to recognize something equally important: the knowledge and experience that help the industry navigate challenges, improve operations, and prepare for the future.

Insight is another pillar of FRA’s work, reflected in its quarterly Technical Releases (TRs), which bring together research, industry experiences, practical guidance, and emerging technologies to inform and support professionals throughout the wood supply chain.

Technical Releases are an FRA member benefit, and members must log in to the FRA website to access them. FRA also occasionally makes select releases publicly available when the information has broader value to the industry. One recent example is 26-TR-24: Update on Impact of Skyrocketing Fuel Prices on Forest Products Transportation, which FRA made available to the public because of the widespread impact of rising diesel costs on the wood supply chain.

FRA’s 10 latest Technical Releases highlight the breadth of issues affecting the wood supply chain, from rising fuel costs and mill viability to employee retention, forest management, and new approaches to industry communication.

Transportation Costs and Business Planning

26-TR-24: Update on Impact of Skyrocketing Fuel Prices on Forest Products Transportation

Rising diesel prices have significant consequences for transporting raw forest products. Based on the study’s assumptions, the increase in diesel prices between September 2025 and September 2026 is estimated to have added an average of $2.12 per green ton to delivered wood costs, equivalent to more than $920 million in additional annual transportation fuel costs across the sector. The findings demonstrate how energy costs affect businesses throughout the wood supply chain.

26-TR-28: Using the Futures Market to Budget for Diesel Price

Predicting diesel costs can be difficult, but futures market data can offer useful guidance. This release explains how businesses can combine publicly available diesel futures prices with historical regional pricing information to develop more informed fuel budgets. Although futures prices do not guarantee future costs, they provide an alternative to relying solely on current prices or guesswork.

People, Safety, and Industry Communication

26-TR-25: Telling the Forest Story: Social Media’s Role in Public Education and Engagement

Social media provides an opportunity for forestry professionals to engage directly with the public and share the realities of modern forest management. Drawing on the University of Idaho’s experience reaching millions of viewers, this release demonstrates how digital storytelling can educate audiences, address misconceptions, and introduce new generations to the forest products industry.

26-TR-26: Wood & Iron: A Logging Podcast, For Loggers. By Loggers.

Produced by the University of Wisconsin-Stevens Point’s Wisconsin Forestry Center, Wood & Iron brings firsthand logging experiences to an accessible podcast format. Covering business management, workforce development, equipment, safety, and changing market conditions, the program offers logging professionals an opportunity to learn from their peers and exchange ideas across regions.

26-TR-31: Promoting Camera Technology to Improve Safety in the Mississippi Log Trucking Industry

Truck-mounted cameras can provide footage to support driver training, document incidents, and identify driving practices that warrant attention. This release shares findings from three Mississippi workshops attended by 70 business owners and employees. It also identifies the installation and subscription costs that can discourage adoption, illustrating both the benefits and practical challenges of introducing new safety technology.

26-TR-32: Retention Strategies Leading Wood Products Employers Are Using to Keep Their Best People

Retaining experienced employees is increasingly important as wood products manufacturers face labor shortages and competition for skilled workers. This release outlines strategies beyond competitive compensation, including clear career pathways, stronger frontline leadership, employee development, cross-training, and preventing burnout. Its central message is that successful retention requires sustained investment in people.

Forest Management, Markets, and Innovation

26-TR-27: Forest Innovation Platform, a Forest Information Tool

Developed by American Forests, the Forest Innovation Platform provides free access to more than 40 datasets that can support forest management decisions. Users can evaluate information such as timber volume, forest conditions, wildfire risk, and projected environmental changes for specific geographic areas. The tool demonstrates how accessible data can help land managers better understand forest resources and potential risks.

26-TR-29: Techno-Economic Viability Assessment of the U.S. Wood-Consuming Pulp & Paper Industry

Pulp and paper mills serve as important markets for wood fiber, and their closures can affect the entire supply chain. Highlighting an analysis conducted by AFRY for the U.S. Endowment for Forestry & Communities, this release reports that AFRY’s assessment identifies nearly 20 U.S. pulp and paper mills at elevated or moderate risk of closure in the medium term, representing approximately 30 million green tons of annual wood demand. The findings underscore the importance of mill viability to forest management, logging operations, and other wood-consuming businesses.

26-TR-30: What to Do if You Encounter a Cemetery or Other Areas of Importance in the Forest

Forest management sometimes reveals historic cemeteries, former homesites, and other culturally or locally significant features. This release offers practical guidance for identifying, documenting, marking, and protecting these areas before and during forestry operations. It emphasizes communication with landowners, awareness of applicable requirements, and careful planning to preserve important sites.

26-TR-33: FY26 NEPA Efficiency and Timber Volume Positioning

The USDA Forest Service reports improvements in environmental review efficiency across Pacific Regions 5, 6, and 10. During fiscal year 2026, the regions signed 98 National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) decisions, with environmental assessment decision times improving by 34% compared with FY25. The release also highlights efforts to coordinate environmental planning with future timber sale schedules, helping position the regions to deliver more predictable timber supply.

While these releases cover very different subjects, they share a common purpose: making valuable information available to the people who can put it to use.

A logging contractor evaluating new safety technology, a mill manager considering workforce retention strategies, or a forester exploring digital mapping tools may face different day-to-day decisions. Yet each operates within a connected industry where changes in one sector can affect another. FRA’s Technical Releases help bridge those connections by sharing research, operational experiences, and lessons from across the country.

Technical Releases also reflect the diversity of expertise that supports the forest products industry. Researchers, foresters, logging professionals, manufacturers, transportation specialists, and industry partners all contribute knowledge that can help businesses make informed decisions, improve operations, and navigate changing industry conditions.

As we celebrate National Forest Products Week, we recognize not only the essential products, jobs, and economic contributions of the industry, but also the collaboration and shared expertise that support its continued success.
