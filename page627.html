#8041. The Public School System Has Failed American Kids. Now What?

📅 16 Oct 2026 | Category: other | Source

Writing in the London Times on Sept. 29, author Evan Barker wrote a sobering account about her experience in a Kansas City, Missouri, school two decades ago:

Every day is a fight for survival, not a place to get an education. During passing periods, I’d hurry as fast as I could to avoid being 'jumped.’ I’d walk down the hallways seeing fights break out with alarms blaring and the school’s lone police officer running through masses of pupils after a kid had been beaten up. It’s impossible to learn when you’re constantly in survival mode.

The next day, the Daily Mail published a piece headlined “Huge room full of distraught children confront school district over violent behavior at chaotic New York school.” This was not a troubled New York City school, but a high school in upstate Henrietta, south of Rochester. Students “claim the entire student body has been desensitized to fights, substance use and physical threats.”

This is useful context for Nicole Hannah-Jones’ recent reentry into the national conversation. In her article “In Fighting for Every Black Child, Did I Betray My Own?” she discusses her daughter’s experience in a New York City school. In brief: absent or incompetent teachers; misbehaving and violent students; kids who can’t read or write.

>>> France’s Education Collapse Is a Warning for America

Ms. Hannah-Jones deliberately placed her daughter in New York’s Public School 307, hoping the presence of enlightened parents such as herself would make them better. It didn’t. Neither will more money. Her daughter’s elementary school spends $52,000 per pupil, so each classroom costs taxpayers more than a million bucks a year.

For that, they should expect teachers who show up, grade papers and keep kids under control. They also could hope for better than 17% proficiency in math and 28% in reading at a school with a ratio of only nine pupils per teacher.

Harvard economists Will Dobbie and Roland Fryer found that class size, per-pupil expenditure, teacher certifications and advanced degrees are not correlated with school effectiveness. What is? “Frequent teacher feedback, the use of data to guide instruction, high-dosage tutoring, increased instructional time and high expectations.” The proof of this concept is in the field.

In New York, charter schools—where discipline and accountability are possible—outperform regular city schools in reading and math. These are not bastions of “white privilege,” but highly diverse schools with 95% non-white students. In London, Katherine Birbalsingh’s Michaela School continually churns out top exam results, with a student body that is mostly non-White and non-wealthy.

As a former teacher, my heart sinks for the millions of American kids held captive to left-wing ideology. Their teachers no doubt read Paulo Freire’s “Pedagogy of the Oppressed” in teacher college, because research shows it is one of the most often assigned books in colleges of education. There, they learned not so much how to teach but how to foment activism.

Many of the good teachers move to better schools or simply give up teaching. The worst stay, protected by their labor unions from ever being fired.

>>> Stop Writing Blank Checks to Campuses That Protect Hate

The national teachers’ unions want lifetime employment with no accountability. They spend millions to elect politicians who will pay them more and expect less. They resist efforts to fire teachers for poor work or conduct, implement performance pay, or test teachers’ competence and knowledge. And of course, they most bitterly resist charter schools, school choice, or other attempts to give parents a choice to escape failed schools in the public K-12 system.

Meanwhile, the National Assessment of Educational Progress reveals that students have made no gains since they began tracking performance 30 years ago. iPads, PCs and now artificial intelligence are not magic bullets that can replace study and discipline. Faddish nonsense such as Critical Race Theory, equitable grading, emotional and social learning, ethnic studies, and multicultural awareness takes valuable, limited time away from reading, writing, math and science.

We owe it to our children to end this expensive failure and resume what works. As I wrote in “The Ten Woke Commandments (You Must Not Obey)”, the solutions are obvious, but the teachers’ unions will fight them to the death: no phones in class, student discipline, testing, teacher competency, longer hours, more homework and parental responsibility.

It’s not rocket science. But if we fix K-12, maybe we’ll get some more rocket scientists out of the wasted talent stagnating in schools such as P.S. 307.

#3873. Simulation App Speeds Up Electric Truck Battery Design

📅 16 Oct 2026 | Category: other | Source


This article is brought to you by COMSOL.

These days, no one blinks an eye at two-day, one-day, and even same-day package deliveries. Despite the plethora of localized fulfillment and distribution centers that have cropped up to meet the demand, delivery trucks still need to make considerably long drives, often over highways. To reduce fuel and maintenance costs as well as carbon emissions, companies are investing in electric trucks for their delivery fleets. While turning to electric trucks is a promising solution, the battery packs incorporated into these automotive designs often fall short in the lifetime needed to make long-distance highway travel feasible.

To help overcome the issue of battery lifetime, a consortium of universities and industry partners incorporated multiscale simulation and a custom simulation app into the battery manufacturing process. The organizations involved in this project were Aurobay, Resolvent, the European Commission, the Technical University of Denmark (DTU), Vinnova (the Swedish Agency for Innovation Systems), Innovation Fund Denmark, and Volvo Group Trucks Operations.

The simulation app, built by Resolvent, a Denmark-based engineering consultancy that specializes in green energy, enables the R&D department at Volvo to easily test battery lifetime performance.

LaserWho?

The Multiscale Simulation of Laser Welding for Optimal Battery Pack Manufacturing project — referred to as “LaserBATMAN” — was initiated in May 2022 and wrapped up in May 2025 (Ref. 1). Although BATMAN here refers to battery manufacturing and not the famous masked superhero, the goals for the project also strove for greatness.

The LaserBATMAN project aimed to optimize two key performance metrics for battery packs: robustness and lifetime (Ref. 1). One element of the battery manufacturing process that affects both of these metrics is the laser welding used in component joining processes (for example, to connect battery cells in a pack via a busbar). If done correctly, laser welding can positively impact battery life, but if done incorrectly, laser welding can increase the risk of battery degradation. Computational modeling can be used to analyze and predict the effects of welding and joining processes at different stages of the manufacturing process.

Martin Refslund Nielsen, former chief commercial officer and partner at Resolvent, explained that for this type of multiphysics and multiscale problem, “COMSOL Multiphysics is just the right tool.”

R&D Workflow for Multiscale Model and App

The Resolvent team used the COMSOL Multiphysics software to study how laser welding affects the electrochemistry and degradation of battery cells. At least, that’s how the project started.

“Ambitions grew,” Nielsen said, explaining that the team ended up modeling the performance of the entire battery, from cell to pack level, and even “turned [the model] into a design tool in the end”, in reference to the app.

After training the deep neural network of the battery model, the team built a custom simulation app to efficiently optimize busbar tabs in the configuration of a battery pack.

First, the team built an electrochemical model of a battery cell by inputting the formulas for the relevant chemical reaction kinetics (Figure 2). The resulting cell-layer model was then fed input data, and the output data was used to train a deep neural network (DNN). With this reduced-order model, the team was able to quickly gain insight into cell behavior.

After training the DNN of the battery model, the team built a custom simulation app to efficiently optimize busbar tabs in the configuration of a battery pack.

Figure 2: The Resolvent battery model.COMSOL

A “Tool Within a Tool” for Busbar Weld Design

The different features of the simulation app, as described by Nielsen, include its options to input dimensions for the pack geometry (Figure 3), the number of cells, and operating conditions such as ambient temperature and charge–discharge current profiles. The app also makes it possible to apply a simple charging current and load customized cycles. Users are then able to see how the battery performs with each set of specific inputs.

Figure 3. The battery pack model developed by Resolvent.COMSOL

Another important aspect of Resolvent’s app is the Busbar Weld Design Tool button, which Nielsen described as “a tool within a tool.” This button can be used to efficiently calculate busbar weld performance after a number of cycles. After clicking the button, a window opens with options for setting different parameters, including the shape of the weld, busbar thickness, and penetration depth of the laser (Figure 4).

“[This button] is interesting from a robust design perspective,” said Nielsen. Users can apply a variant to the input parameters to see what happens in production if the weld does not reach far enough into the busbar.

Figure 4. Resolvent’s simulation app, showing how certain parameters can be customized. The model shows how the busbar weld is affected by a cooling airflow.COMSOL

“We certainly get deep technical insight here, and we can dimension the design in the sweet spot. Deep technical insight is also key to optimizing product performance,” Nielsen said. For example, if the results suggest better cooling, the team could potentially reduce the design by two battery cells and retain the same performance, easily quantifying the tradeoff between cooling and setup.

Predicting Pack Performance

The app’s user interface also includes a Compute Battery button, which can be used to see the temperature of the weld in a given state, the state of charge, and the solid–electrolyte interface (SEI) layer thickness (Figure 5). The results can help the engineering team further determine if there is room for optimization in the design. For instance, a user can apply a cooling flow in the app and see if turbulence or standing air occurs.

As explained by Nielsen, simulation apps offer more consistent analysis and reporting, and the intuitiveness of custom apps makes it easier to share knowledge. “We are in a much better place to optimize performance and increase robustness,” said Nielsen.

Figure 5. Temperature in the given state (left), state of charge (middle), and SEI layer thickness (right) in the battery pack.COMSOL

The custom simulation app developed by the LaserBATMAN consortium can be used to help produce more robust battery designs with ranges that can support highway travel over longer distances with fewer failures — all while promoting the use of green energy. A heroic project indeed.
